# Why The Interior is built the way it is

*The long version, for people who like reasons.*

## Kept, not scrolled

A feed is a machine for spending attention. It studies what you love so it can keep you looking, and it never ends, because ending is the one thing it cannot afford. The Interior begins from the opposite premise: that what moves you is worth **keeping**, and that a record kept carefully enough starts to reflect a person back to themselves.

So there is no feed. There are no likes, no counts of anyone but you, no notifications. The app does not summon you; you visit it, the way you would return to a notebook.

## Your taste is the only algorithm

When you want something new, The Interior does not consult a model of "people like you". It reads *your* archive — the concerts you stood through, the books you copied lines from, the one exhibition that disturbed you — and goes looking in the real world: actual programmes, actual dates, actual booking pages. Every recommendation names the entry it grew from, so you can always see the reasoning. It arrives as a short letter, and the letter ends. You close it, and you choose.

## One person at a time

This is personal software in the old sense: built for one reader, tuned for their interior life. It has no growth curve, no community features, no reason to be sticky. That is not a limitation to be fixed later; it is the design. If you want one, you run your own — your data lives in your own database, under your own key.

## The life of an event

An enthusiasm has a life cycle, and the app follows it rather than flattening it into a list:

- **Discovered** — found by the letter, or by you.
- **Saved** — it waits in the Antechamber, the room for things not yet lived.
- **In hand** — you have the ticket, or the book is on the table. A companion sheet appears: background to read before, spoiling nothing.
- **Lived** — you went. One tap turns the want into an entry, already filled in.
- **Inscribed** — it takes its place in the Ledger, folio-numbered, part of your taste now.

What the archive found for you becomes the archive.

## Restraint as a feature

Warm paper, a vermilion rubric, a variable serif, hairline borders. Nothing louder than the words. Every choice that could be quieter is quieter — because the point of the whole thing is to make room for an interior life, and rooms are made of what you leave out.
